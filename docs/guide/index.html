Start the app

Atlas runs from its project folder in development mode, which reloads screen changes by itself.

cd ~/Projects/atlas
pnpm tauri:dev

When to restart

  • Screen changes appear live while pnpm tauri:dev is running.
  • Changes to the Rust side (new commands, the index): stop with Ctrl C and run pnpm tauri:dev again. The first rebuild takes a minute or two.
  • New packages: stop, run pnpm install, then pnpm tauri:dev.

The first time, choose your vault folder. Atlas remembers it and reopens it next time. The search index rebuilds itself when it needs to.

Finding your way

The sidebar lists everything; each page opens in a panel with a bar across the top.

Sidebar, top

Search ⌘K, Today, Inbox, Graph, Tags, Automations, Activity, Templates and Terms. Counts show how many notes each holds; Activity's red badge counts new errors.

Favorites

Star any page (the ☆ in its top bar) to pin it here.

Types · Views · Dashboards

Built from your vault. The + beside a heading makes a new one.

Pages

Your folders and notes. Near the end, Archive opens the Archive; System, last, is the .atlas folder (types, automations, sources, settings). Templates aren't in it — they have their own page.

Arrange the sidebar

  • One scroll for the sections. The vault's name, the links at the top (Search, Today, Inbox and the rest) and the footer (Settings, the light/dark switch, sync) stay put. Everything from the first section to the last — Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards and Pages, in your order — scrolls together in the one area between them, and each open section shows all its rows; no section has a scrollbar of its own.
  • Headings stay in sight. As you scroll through a section, its heading (with its grip and +) stays pinned at the top until the next section's heading takes its place.
  • Open or close a section by clicking its heading (Favorites, Types, Views, Dashboards or Pages). A shut section stays exactly where it is in your order; only its rows fold away, so nothing below it jumps past it.
  • Put the sections in any order. Hover over a heading, or tab to it, and a grip appears just to its left (Move Favorites, Move Pages and so on). Drag the grip, or focus it and press ⌥↑ / ⌥↓ to move the section one place. Open or shut, every section moves in the same one order.
  • The order belongs to the vault. It's saved in .atlas/settings.md, so it follows the vault to another Mac, and each vault keeps its own.
If .atlas/settings.md can't be read (say its frontmatter was broken by a hand edit), Atlas shows a notice naming the file and leaves it alone until it is fixed; the sections can't be moved until then. If a move can't be saved, a notice says The sidebar's order could not be saved and why.

The top bar of a page

  • ← → Back and Forward, or ⌘[ ⌘], or your mouse's side buttons. Each panel keeps its own history.
  • Breadcrumb shows where the page lives.
  • Saved / Saving… / Unsaved is the save status. Notes save as you type; ⌘S saves at once.
  • Spark button: Ask Claude (⌘J) — see Claude in Atlas.
  • Split button, just before the …: Split right (⇧⌘\). See Two pages side by side below.
  • … menu: Link to…, Show in graph, Move to…, Archive (or Unarchive), Delete…, Split right, Close pane, and more for views and dashboards.
  • × Close pane, at the very end of the bar, once the window is split.

In a narrow window the bar gives way a step at a time: first the folder goes and the note's name shortens, then Saved hides, then the Back and Forward arrows (⌘[ ⌘] still work). The × and the … always stay.

Two pages side by side

  • Split with the split button in the page's bar, next to the …; its tooltip says Split right (⇧⌘\). The new pane opens on the same note. Or press ⇧⌘\, or choose Split right from the … menu.
  • Two panes at most. Once the window is split, the split button goes.
  • Each pane has a × at the end of its bar that closes that pane. The other pane keeps its note; close the left one and the right one moves over.
  • ⇧⌘\ closes the pane you're in, so its hint shows on that pane's × only. Close pane in the … menu does the same.
  • Nothing you type is lost. What you typed in a pane as it closes is saved, and if the other pane shows the same note, it picks the change up.
  • Each pane keeps its own Back and Forward, and the split is still there after a restart.

Hide the sidebar with ⌘\. Settings and the light/dark switch sit at the bottom of the sidebar (⌘,), beside the sync light once sync is set up. ⌘J opens Claude in a pane beside the page.

Notes

A note is a markdown file. Its name is the title you type at the top of the page.

Make one

  • ⌘N makes a note in the folder you're looking at, or at the top level. Made while you're looking at an archived note, it goes at the top level, not in the Archive.
  • Right-click a folder in Pages → New note here.
  • On a type's page, New <type> (New person, New book) — on the toolbar or at the foot of the table — makes a note of that type from its template.
  • The round + button in the corner, or ⌥⌘N, adds a note of one of your chosen types without leaving the page — see The add button.

Write

Type markdown as you go (# for headings, - for lists, [] for a checkbox) or type / for the block menu: headings, lists, task list, quote, code block, callout, table, divider, Link to note and Image.

A link to a note can sit on its own line as a card with the note's picture and summary — see Bookmarks.

# with a space after it is a heading; # straight followed by a word is a tag, like #idea — see Tags. @ and a name links a person — see People and mentions.

Properties

Properties sit under the title as rows: a status pill, dates like Tue, Sep 22, 2026, numbers, links to other notes.

  • + Add a property asks for a name and a kind (text, number, date, checkbox, select, multi-select, URL, relation, thumbnail). On a typed note choose Add to <Type> to add it to every note of that type, or Only this note.
  • Relations show the linked notes as chips; click one to open it, × to remove it. A relation set to Several notes keeps an Add… button.
  • A relation's dropdown (— no company — until one is chosen) lists only real notes of the type it points at — never a template, and nothing in .atlas.
  • New <type>… at the bottom of the dropdown (New company…) makes one on the spot: type a name in Name of the new company and press Return. Atlas makes a new note of that type from its template — the template itself is left untouched — and links it. It goes at the top level of the vault (a task goes beside the note, as with the add button), numbered if the name is taken. Esc cancels.
  • A name a link couldn't reach — one holding #, ^, [ or ] — is refused, with the reason: “Acme #1” cannot be linked: a link stops at #. So is a name with nothing left once the characters a file name can't hold (/, :, | and the like) are turned to spaces: Give the new company a name. Nothing is made either way.
  • Dates save when you leave the field or press Return. Use Clear to remove one.
Title and file name. Editing the title at the top renames the file, unless the note has a title property, in which case it edits that. Clearing a title that came from the property removes it.

Pictures in notes

Put a picture anywhere in a note. Atlas copies it into your vault and writes an ordinary markdown image, so Obsidian shows it too.

Paste

A screenshot or a copied image file: ⌘V in the note.

Drop

Drag image files from Finder onto the note. They land where you drop them.

Block menu

Type / → Image and pick a file. It goes where you typed.

Page menu

… in the top bar → Insert image…

  • While a picture is saving, a placeholder holds its place. If it can't be saved, the reason shows there and nothing is added to the note.
  • Click a picture to select it, then type its alt text (a short description) in the Describe the image field under it. Return or Esc goes back to writing.
  • PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP and SVG work, and HEIC when the Mac can draw it. Each can be up to 20 MB. Two pictures with the same name are numbered, never overwritten.
  • Deleting a picture from the note removes it from the note only. The file stays in the vault.
  • Moving a note: Update N links fixes its pictures' paths too.

Where they go: Settings (⌘,) → Images → Images go in: the Attachments folder (attachments/, the default) or Beside the note, in the note's own folder.

Not yet: resizing a picture, and showing Obsidian's ![[picture.png]] embeds as pictures. Those are kept exactly as written, so Obsidian still shows them.

The add button

A round + floats over your work. It adds a note of a type you use often — a task by default — and leaves you where you were.

  • One type chosen: press it (or ⌥⌘N) and a small box asks straight away, e.g. New task…
  • Two to five types: press it and a short list slides out; pick one.
  • The box asks for a title and up to three things worth filling in on the way — whatever the type requires, then its status, what it links to (a task's project) and its first date. Return adds it and keeps you on your page; ⌘ Return adds it and opens it. Esc closes it.
  • After adding, a message offers Open.
  • Tasks go in the folder you're looking at, as ⌘N does — at the top level if that's the Archive; other types go at the top level of the vault.

Choose its types

Settings (⌘,) → Quick add. Use Add a type… to add one (five at most), drag to reorder, and the × beside one to remove it. With none chosen, the button hides.

Move it

Drag it anywhere; when you let go it snaps to the nearest of eight places — the four corners, the middle of the top and bottom edges, and the middle of the left and right sides. From the keyboard, focus it and press ⌥ + an arrow. Atlas remembers where you left it.

Folders, moving and deleting

Right-click (or hover and press …) on anything in Pages.

  • New folder, New note here, Rename, Move to…, Archive, Delete… — on a folder, Archive N notes; see Archive.
  • Drag a note or folder onto a folder to move it. From the keyboard: select a row, Space to pick it up, arrows, Space to drop.
  • After a rename or move, Atlas says how many links still use the old name and offers Update N links. Nothing is rewritten until you click it.
  • Delete sends things to the macOS Trash, so you can drag them back. The dialog says how many notes and other files go with a folder, and warns about unsaved edits.
  • Views and dashboards are deleted from their own … menu. They stay in their sections and can't be moved.
  • The Archive has rules of its own. Nothing can be moved into it — “Archive a note from its menu, so it can go back where it was.” — and nothing in it can be moved out except by Unarchive. Nothing can be moved or renamed to become the top-level Archive folder: “Only archiving puts notes in the Archive — choose another name.” And the Archive folder can't be deleted in one go: “Delete notes from the Archive instead.”

Archive

Put a note away without deleting it. It leaves your lists, searches and graph, and can be found and brought back at any time.

Archive a note

Page menu

… in the note's top bar → Archive.

Pages

Right-click a note → Archive. On a folder, Archive N notes archives every note in it; the folder and any other files stay put.

Search

⌘K → Archive this note, for the note you're on. Open Archive is there too.

Several at once

In a table view, Select, tick the rows, then Archive N notes — see Views.

  • It moves to Archive/ at the path it had — Projects/Plan.md becomes Archive/Projects/Plan.md, numbered if that is taken — and gets archived: <today> and archivedFrom: <where it was> in its properties. Nothing else in the file changes.
  • If the note already had its own archived or archivedFrom property, it is kept aside and given back, exactly as written, when the note comes out.
  • Links: archiving one note offers Update N links, as a move does. Archiving several updates the links to them by itself.
  • A note with unsaved typing is saved first, then archived.
  • On a schedule: an automation can archive the notes a query finds — the ready-made one archives done tasks after 30 days. It leaves a note with unsaved typing where it is rather than save it, and its last run can be undone.
  • A note more than 32 folders deep can't be archived: “It is too many folders deep: in the Archive, Atlas would no longer read it.” If some notes in a batch can't go, a message names each one and why.

Out of the way

  • In Pages the whole Archive is one Archive row; click it to open the Archive page rather than a long folder.
  • Archived notes are left out of views and type counts, search, the graph, [[ suggestions and note pickers, the Tags page's counts, and the Links panel's links in and unlinked mentions.
  • To reach them anyway: Include archived in search (⌘K) and on a view's toolbar, and the Archived switch on the graph. On a view it turns itself off when you go to another view; search starts without it each time.
  • Renaming a tag still changes it in archived notes, so they read right when they come back.

The Archive page

Open it from the Archive row in Pages, or ⌘K → Open Archive. It lists archived notes, most recently archived first, with where each Came from and the day it was Archived.

  • Search the Archive… matches notes whose title or path holds every word you type, in any capitals — überblick finds Überblick, however the Mac stored its accent. It shows up to 500 at a time; with more, it says More are archived; search to narrow them.
  • Click a name to open the note. Unarchive on its row puts it back.
  • Tick several and press Unarchive N notes. Any that couldn't go back stay ticked, with the reason in a message.

Unarchive

  • From the Archive page, the note's own … menu (Unarchive), or ⌘K → Unarchive this note. Several at once: on the Archive page, or in a table view with Include archived and Select on.
  • It goes back to where the Archive holds it, minus Archive/: Archive/Projects/Plan.md goes to Projects/Plan.md, numbered if something has taken that place since. The archived and archivedFrom properties come off.
  • Editing archivedFrom doesn't change where it goes: that record only spells the same place as the note had it.

Types and properties

A type is a kind of note — Task, Project, Person — with its own properties. Types live as files in .atlas/types.

  1. Press + next to Types in the sidebar, give it a name and an icon.
  2. Open the type's definition with Edit type — see Edit a type below.
  3. Add properties, choose each kind, drag to reorder, mark required ones.
  4. For a Relation, choose the type it points at, and One note or Several notes.
  5. For a Thumbnail, nothing more to choose: each note of the type gets a picture of its own page (see Thumbnails). A type can have only one.

Edit a type

Clicking a type in the sidebar opens its views, not its definition. To change the type itself, any of these opens Edit type:

  • The gear at the end of the type's view tabs (Edit Task type, for Task).
  • Right-click the type in the sidebar (or focus it and press ⇧F10) → Edit type. The same menu has Open.
  • Search ⌘K: type edit and pick Edit <type> type, e.g. Edit Task type. These only appear once what you type starts with “edit”, so they never crowd out notes.

Each of these places also has Edit template, for what new notes of the type start as — see Templates.

On the type's page, the Notes side of the Notes | Edit type switch goes back to its views.

Options for select properties

Rename, reorder, colour and remove options — for example the task statuses backlog, next, doing, review, done. Renaming an option asks “N notes use ‘review’…” and offers to update them. Order sets the order of a board's columns and lanes, and of a table's groups. The Done option decides which status a ticked checkbox sets.

Built-in types

Person, Task, Project, Artifact, Meeting, Company and Term are marked Built in: Atlas's own features depend on them. On Edit type their Delete button is turned off, with the reason beside it — for example “Person is built in — @ mentions find people by it — so it cannot be deleted. Its properties are yours to change.” Their properties stay yours to edit. Their files in .atlas/types can't be deleted or renamed from Pages either, whatever the file is called: “Atlas’s built-in types stay — its features depend on them.”

A type you made has Delete <Type>… at the foot of Edit type; it asks first, as deleting any note does, and puts the type's file in the Trash.

Projects

Atlas ships a Project type (status: planned, active, paused, done; owner; due; summary). Every task has a project relation, and the Projects view is a board grouped by project.

Templates

A template is what a new note starts as — its properties and its text. Editing one changes the notes made from it next, never the ones already made.

What uses a template

A template's name is what ties it to what it makes:

  • A type's template is called what the type is called — Person for Person, in any capitals. Every new note of the type starts from it: New person on the type's page and at the foot of its table, the add button, Create person “…”.
  • Daily is what Today's note starts as, Task what a captured task starts as, and Artifact what a saved artifact starts as.
  • Any other template is only offered in the New menu — the + beside the vault's name, under Blank note, Today's note and Claude artifact….

The Templates page

Sidebar

Templates, in the top list between Automations and Activity.

Search

⌘K → Templates.

From a template

All templates on the band over any template you're editing.

It lists every template with what it is Used for: New Person notes, Today’s note, Captured tasks, Saved artifacts, or Only the New menu. Each row has:

  • Edit (or click its name) opens it.
  • Rename turns the name into a field: Return or clicking away renames it, Esc leaves it. Since the name is the tie, a new name can cut it loose, and the field says so while you type — “New Person notes will start empty.” or “Today’s note will start blank.”
  • Move to notes… — see A template that was really a note below.
  • Delete… asks “Move “Meeting template” to the Trash?”, with the same warning of what stops using it, and puts the file in the macOS Trash.

New template, at the top of the page, asks what it's For:

  • <Type> notes, for a type that has no template yet. Its name is filled in and can't be changed — “Named after the type, so new Book notes start from it.” It starts with type: and each of the type's properties, empty.
  • No type — a blank template, named as you like, for the New menu.

A template's name can't hold / \ : * ? " < > |, start or end with a dot, or be one another template already has.

Edit a type's template

Edit template sits beside Edit type, everywhere it is:

  • The template button just after the gear on a type's view tabs (Edit Person template, for Person).
  • Right-click the type in the sidebar → Edit template.
  • Edit template at the top of the type's own page, beside Notes | Edit type.
  • Search ⌘K: type edit and pick Edit <type> template, e.g. Edit Person template.

If the type has no template, Atlas makes one first — named after the type, holding type: and each of its properties with nothing filled in — and opens it.

Editing a template

  • It opens like a note — properties, then text — under a band: “Template · Used for: New Person notes. Editing it changes what new notes start as — never the notes already made.” The band has Move to notes… and All templates.
  • There's no favourite star (it would be copied into every new note), and no Move, Archive or graph. Renaming its title renames the template, with the same tie as above.
  • The Dashboard template shows as a note, not a dashboard; what you make from it is the dashboard.

Templates are never notes

  • They aren't in Pages (not even under System), search, the graph, [[ and @ suggestions, relation pickers, links in or out, or unlinked mentions. A template's tags don't count on the Tags page.
  • A link that used to reach a template — [[Person]] once opened the Person template — now shows as missing, or reaches a note of that name if there is one.

A template that was really a note

If a note ended up in the templates folder — say a company saved over the Company template — Move to notes…, on its row or its band, asks “Make “Larkspur Payroll” a note?”: “It moves out of the templates to the top of the vault, exactly as it is. Links to “Larkspur Payroll” reach it again.” It's numbered if a note there has its name, and it says what stops using it, if anything. Then press Move to notes.

Views

A view is a saved way of looking at notes of one type, and a type owns its views. Click a type in the sidebar and its views open as tabs across the top. To look across several types at once, save a query as a view.

A type's views, as tabs

  • Click a type in the sidebar and you see only that type's views, as tabs. It opens on the tab you last had for that type on this Mac, else the first.
  • Opening a view any other way — a link, search, the Views section — lands on the same page, with that tab selected.
  • A type with no views yet shows one default table tab, named for the type (Idea table), with every property as a column. It is only written as a view once you add a view beside it, rename it or duplicate it. It can't be deleted — there's nothing to delete.

Add, rename, copy and delete from the tabs

  • + at the end of the tabs (Add a view) lists the layouts; pick one. The view is named for you, like Task board or Task list (then Task list 2), and opens selected.
  • The … on the selected tab (View options) has:
    • Rename — type the new name in the tab and press Return. It changes the tab's name only; you never need to know the view's file name, and links to it keep working.
    • Duplicate — a full copy, with its filter, sort and grouping, named … copy (Board copy, then Board copy 2).
    • Delete view — asks first, then Move to Trash. You land on the next tab, or the previous one if it was last.
  • Settings are edited in place: Layout, Filter, Sort, Group and Properties in the toolbar, as below.

Reorder the tabs

  • Drag a tab to a new place, or focus it and press Alt+← / Alt+→ to move it one place.
  • The order is saved in the views themselves, so it syncs to your other Macs.
  • Views nobody has reordered keep the old order (by layout, then name), and nothing is rewritten until you change the tabs.

Query views, SQL views and dashboards are unchanged: they belong to no one type, so they keep their own pages and their places under Views and Dashboards.

Make and change views

  • New view: the + on a type's tabs (above), or the + next to Views in the sidebar — pick a name, the type and a layout. That sidebar + also has New query, for a view across types — see Queries.
  • Layout, Filter, Sort, Group and Properties (which fields show) change the view at once. Grouping is below.
  • While it differs from what's saved, the toolbar shows Reset · Save as new view… · Save view. Editing cells, dragging cards and ticking tasks save straight away.
  • Include archived lists archived notes too, for now. It isn't saved with the view and turns off when you go to another view.

Group and sub-group

  • Group in the toolbar has Group by, then Then by to split each group again. It works on tables and boards: on a table they're groups and sub-groups, on a board they're columns and lanes.
  • None turns a grouping off. A board always has columns, so its Group by has no None; its Then by does.
  • A property that can hold several values — a multi-select, a relation set to Several notes — is listed but can't be picked, with the reason: “A note can have several of labels, so it cannot be grouped by it.”
  • Groups come in order: a select's options as the type orders them, numbers by size, dates by day, the rest by name — reversed when the view sorts that property descending. Notes with no value gather under No value, last.
  • A checkbox groups into true (ticked) and false, which holds every note not ticked, including those without the box.

Grouped tables

  • Each group gets a heading row across the table: an arrow, the group's value (a coloured pill for a status or select), and how many notes. Sub-groups sit one step in, with headings of their own.
  • The heading row shows totals under the columns: a number column's sum, and for a checkbox how many are ticked (2 checked).
  • Click a heading to fold the group shut or open it again. Folds are remembered per view, on this Mac.
  • + New at the foot of a group adds a note already in it: it gets the group's value, and the sub-group's (a number as a number; the ticked group ticks the box). No value and the unticked group give it nothing.
  • A table switched from a board keeps the board's grouping, so its columns become groups.

Board lanes

  • With Then by set, the board splits into lanes running across all the columns, one per value, each with a heading, a count and an arrow to fold it.
  • Drag a card to any cell — another column, another lane, or both. Both properties change together, as one ⌘Z. Dropping in a No value column or lane takes that property off the note. A number column writes a number and a checkbox column ticks or unticks the box, never text. From the keyboard: Space to pick a card up, arrows to move it across columns and up and down lanes, Space to drop.
  • Each cell shows its first cards and + N more, like a column, and has its own + New that fills in both values.
  • A card doesn't show a chip for the lane's property; the lane already says it.
  • Dragging a task into done finishes it, whether done is a column or a lane. When the type has a status, only that status counts — the last column of some other property is just a column. A repeating task rolls on to its next date once, and the other property you dropped it into is kept.

Act on several rows

  1. In a table view, press Select. Each row gets a checkbox; the one in the heading ticks them all.
  2. Tick the notes. A bar says how many are selected.
  3. Press Archive N notes — or, for archived rows shown with Include archived, Unarchive N notes. One batch runs at a time; any note that couldn't go stays ticked.

Leaving select mode, or the view, clears the ticks.

Calendar

A calendar view places each note on the day its date property names. A note with no date isn't on the calendar.

The toolbar switches between five ranges: Month · Week · 3 days · Day · Agenda. Beside them, ‹ and › step back and forward, and Today jumps to today.

RangeWhat you see
MonthSix weeks of days. A busy day shows +N more; click it for the rest.
Week, 3 days, DayA column per day. Notes with only a date sit in the strip at the top; notes with a time sit on the day's clock, from start to end (half an hour if no end).
AgendaA list: Overdue first, then each day from the one shown with its notes in order. Load more adds another thirty days.
  • Add: click an empty part of a day — or, on the clock, an empty time — to make a note there.
  • Move: drag a note to another day or time. On the clock, drag its bottom edge to change when it ends.
  • Keyboard: click the calendar, then M W 3 D A pick the range, T goes to today, and ← → (or [ ]) step. In the month, Tab to a note, Space to pick it up, arrows to move it a day or a week, Space to drop.

In the agenda, a past note of a type with no “done” status (a meeting, a journal entry) stays under Overdue. That's on purpose for now.

Thumbnails

A picture of a note's page, made by Atlas, that fronts its card in a gallery. Any type can have one.

  1. Add a Thumbnail property: to a whole type in Edit type, or to one note with + Add a property → kind Thumbnail.
  2. That's it. A little after you stop typing (about four seconds, once the note is saved), Atlas takes a picture of the page — title, text and pictures — and redoes it whenever the note changes.
  3. Galleries of that type show the picture on each card.

The Thumbnail row on the note shows the picture, with:

  • Generate / Regenerate — take the picture again now. If the note points at a picture on the web, Atlas asks first, because that one can't be brought back.
  • Choose image… — use a picture of your own instead. It's copied into the vault like a pasted image, and stays until you change it.
  • Clear — no thumbnail. It stays cleared (the row says None) until you press Generate; the gallery falls back to the note's cover or first image.

Pictures Atlas makes are kept in .atlas-cache/thumbnails, not in your notes; the note just says auto. A picture you choose is saved in the vault. Board and list cards don't show thumbnails yet.

Queries

Ask across several types at once — tasks and projects together — follow a link to the note it points at, and sort, group and sub-group the answer. Build it from dropdowns or write it as text; both edit the same query.

  • Open New query from the + next to Views, or from search (⌘K).
  • The page opens on Atlas query, already listing every note of your first type. SQL, the other option beside it, is the SQL page as before.
  • Edit the query as Builder or Text. In the builder, the query it makes is shown as text above the dropdowns.

The builder

  • Fields are the properties of the types in From, plus Title, Type, Tag, Modified and Path, which every note has. A relation's own fields are offered too, as Project · Status: the status of the task's project.
  • What it should be fits the field: is, is not, contains, starts with, is empty, is not empty; numbers add is less than, is at most and so on; dates read is before, is on or after…
  • The value fits the field too: a select's options, the notes a relation can point at (people for an owner), your tags, ticked or not ticked for a checkbox, and for a date either a date that moves — Today, Yesterday, Tomorrow, A week ago, A week from now, A month from now — or On a day… and a date picker. Numbers and words are typed.

Switching between builder and text

  • Switch to Text at any time and carry on typing. Switch back and the builder reads the text again.
  • Text you type is kept as you typed it; the builder's own spelling replaces it only when you change something in the builder.
  • The builder shows conditions that are all joined by AND, or all by OR, each plain or NOT. A query that brackets AND with OR, or puts NOT over a group, stays text, and says why: “This query brackets conditions together — AND with OR, or NOT over a group — which the builder cannot show as one list. Edit it as text.” A query with a mistake in it stays text too, with “Fix the query to open it in the builder: …”
  • SHOW has no dropdown; a SHOW you wrote in text is kept when you go back to the builder.

The answer

  • Layout: Table, Board or List. The count beside it says how many notes, and (more were left out) when the row limit cut it short.
  • Grouped, a table draws the same grouped rows as a grouped table view: heading rows with counts and totals, sub-groups one step in; click a heading to fold it. On a board the groups are the columns and the sub-groups are lanes across them. A query's answer is for reading: it has no + New, and its cards don't drag.
  • Groups come in order: a select's options as the type orders them, numbers by size, dates by day, the rest by name — reversed when the query sorts that field descending — and notes with no value last. A relation's groups are named for the note.
  • A checkbox groups into true (ticked) and false, which holds every note not ticked, including those without the box.
  • Click a note to open it.

Keep it

  • Save as view: a name and a layout, then Save view. It is listed under Views in the sidebar, and a saved query view shows the other saved queries as tabs across the top. A blank query can't be saved: “Write a query before saving: FROM task.”
  • On a saved query view, change the query or the layout and Reset · Save as new view… · Save view appear, as on any view.
  • Add to dashboard: pick the Dashboard, give it a Title if you like, then Add widget. The widget is an Atlas query that lists the rows in their groups and sub-groups, each folding on its own, with relation groups headed by the note's title.
  • The view's file keeps the query as text, exactly as typed, in its query: property.
  • An automation takes its notes with the same builder or text, under Which notes. There, @today and the other moving dates mean the day the rule runs.

Dashboards

  2. Press Customize. Use Add widget, or … → Edit widget on an existing one.
  3. In the side panel choose the kind, the type, what to group by and any filters. The preview updates as you go.

Tasks and daily notes

  • Quick capture ⇧⌘N: type a line and it becomes a task, without leaving what you're doing.
  • Today's note ⇧⌘D opens the daily note, making it if needed.
  • Today and Inbox in the sidebar are views; their counts update as you work.

Links and the graph

  • Link a note by typing [[, a heading or block with [[Note# (see Blocks and embeds), a person by typing @ (see People and mentions), with / → Link to note, or … → Link to…. Any link to a note can also show as a card — see Bookmarks.
  • The Links panel at the foot of a note shows links in and out, including relations like a task's project; on a person's page, the links in are headed Mentioned in. Unlinked mentions finds notes that name this one in plain text; Link turns the mention into a link.
  • Graph (under Search) draws notes as dots coloured by type, sized by how connected they are; relations are dashed. Pan, zoom, drag; click to open, ⌘-click to open beside.
  • … → Show in graph centres it on one note, one or two steps out. Filter by type, hide unlinked notes, or switch to List for the keyboard.
  • Archived notes aren't drawn, and links from them don't count, until you turn on the graph's Archived switch.

Bookmarks

A bookmark is a link to a note shown as a card: the note's picture, its title, a line or two of what it says, and where it lives. It is still an ordinary [[link]] in the file, so Obsidian reads it as one.

Turn a link into a card

From the link

Hover a [[link]] and click the … above it (Link options), or right-click the link, or put the caret on it and press ⇧F10 or the menu key. Choose Show as bookmark.

While linking

In the [[ list, ⇧ Return or a Shift-click puts the note in as a card. The list's foot shows ↵ Link, ⇧ ↵ Bookmark.

  • A link in the middle of a sentence takes its paragraph apart: the words before stay a paragraph, the card follows, the words after go below it.
  • To go back, click the card's … (Options for <title>) → Show as link. You get the link alone in a paragraph.
  • The same menu has Open note.

What the card shows

  • Picture: the note's thumbnail — the one you chose, or the picture of its page once Atlas has made it — else its cover or first image, else an icon. See Thumbnails.
  • Title, as the note's page shows it.
  • Summary: the note's description property, then its summary, then its first paragraph, as plain words. A long one is cut with an ellipsis.
  • Where it lives: the note's folder, and an Archived chip if it's in the Archive.
  • A link to a note that isn't there shows No note is called “X” — it may have been renamed or deleted. A note that can't be read shows This note could not be read just now.
  • [[#Heading]], a link to a heading in the same note, makes a card of that same note.
  • Cards refresh when the note they point to changes.

Use one

  • Click the card to open its note.
  • With a card selected, Return starts a new line after it and ⌘ Return opens the note.

Where cards can go

Cards live only at the top level of a note. Links inside lists, quotes and tables aren't offered Show as bookmark, and a card pasted into a quote, callout, list or table goes in as its plain link.

In the file. A bookmark is the link alone on its line with a marker after it: [[Note]] <!-- atlas:bookmark -->. Obsidian's reading view and GitHub hide the comment; Obsidian's live preview may show it dimmed while the caret is on that line. A plain [[Note]] on its own line stays a link.

Not yet: web links don't become cards. A web link with the marker is kept as written.

Blocks and embeds

Link to one paragraph, list item, task or heading of a note, or show it inside another note, live. Atlas writes these the way Obsidian does, so they work in both.

Link a block

  • Type ![[ and pick a note. Its headings and blocks are offered next, each with a line of preview and its kind (Heading 2, Text, List item, Task, Quote, Callout, Code, Table); typing narrows them. [[Note# does the same for a plain link.
  • Picking writes ![[Note#^id]] for a block or ![[Note#Heading]] for a heading.
  • If the block has no id yet, Atlas adds one (six letters and digits) to its note and changes nothing else there. While that note has unsaved typing it refuses with <Note> has unsaved changes. Save it, then pick again.

The embed

  • ![[Note#^id]] or ![[Note#Heading]] alone on its line shows that block, live and read-only, under its note's name. Change the source and the embed follows.
  • Click the name (Open the block where it is written) to open the note at the block, briefly highlighted. A [[Note#^id]] link does the same.
  • A missing note shows No note is called “X” — it may have been renamed or deleted. A missing block, “Note” has no block ^id — it may have been deleted., or “Note” has no heading “H” any more. One that can't be read, This block could not be read just now.
  • A block from an archived note still shows, with an Archived chip.
  • One level deep: an embed inside a shown block is shown as a link. A note can embed its own blocks too.
  • ![[file.pdf#…]] and other embeds of things that aren't notes stay links.

Block ids

  • Obsidian's ^id, at the end of a paragraph, list item, task or heading, or on its own line after a table, quote, callout or code block. Atlas shows it as a faint ^id after the words.
  • An id follows its block through edits, and when the block becomes a list item, task, quote or heading.
  • A note that already has the same id twice keeps both; when you pick one of them, it gets a new id of its own so the link opens the block you chose.
  • Renaming the source note keeps #^id and #Heading on every link to it.

Not yet: editing an embedded block in place. Open it in its note and edit it there.

People and mentions

Type @ and a name to link a person. A person is a note of the Person type; the mention is an ordinary link, so Obsidian reads it too.

Mention someone

  • @ and a letter lists people: every note with type: person, archived ones left out. A name that matches exactly comes first, then the people you edited most recently. ↓ ↑ choose; Return or a click picks.
  • Picking writes [[Name]] — or the note's path, like [[People/Name]], when another note shares the name — and draws it as a chip with the person's initial. The file keeps plain [[Name]].
  • @ starts a mention at the start of a line or after a space, (, a non-breaking space or an opening quote. Not straight after a letter or digit, so an email like ann@example.com stays as typed, and not in code or inside a link.
  • Names with dots work: @J.R. Hartley finds J.R. Hartley.

Add a new person

  • For a name of up to three words, the list ends with Create person “…”. Return alone never makes one — when it is the only choice, Return is just a new line. Press ↓ to it, then Return, or click it.
  • The new note goes in People/, made from the Person template, numbered if the name is taken there. While it's being made a faded @Name holds its place, then becomes the link.
  • If you change the note before they're made, the link isn't written, and a message says so: “Ann Lee was added to People, but not linked: the note changed while they were being made.”
  • Atlas won't make a person whose note would change what an existing [[link]] opens, or whose name holds # [ ] | or ^, since a link stops there. People who already have such a name are listed greyed out with the reason, like “A|B” cannot be linked: a link stops at |.

Mentioned in

On a person's page the Links panel heads the notes linking to them Mentioned in. Only a note whose type is exactly person counts as a person — for @ and for this heading.

Other spellings of a name

A Person — and a Company — has an Also spelt property (aliases in the file): a nickname, a short form, or how a notetaker mishears the name, commas between them in the properties panel. Atlas spells each of them as the person's name — see Terms.

A vault whose Person or Company type was made before this has no such property yet: add one called aliases on Edit type as a Multi-select, or give the note an aliases list by hand. Atlas reads aliases either way.

Meetings

Your meeting notes come into the vault by themselves. A meeting is a note of the Meeting type, one markdown file per meeting, with the transcript split into turns you can link to and quote.

How a meeting arrives

  1. The meeting ends and Google Meet's “Notes by Gemini” email lands, as it always has.
  2. n8n, on your work computer, reads the email and writes the meeting to Notion's Meeting Notes, as before. Only after the Notion write does it commit the same meeting as one markdown file to your vault's GitHub repository (pkm-space), at Inbox/Meetings/<date> <title>.md — for example Inbox/Meetings/2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync.md.
  3. Atlas pulls the repository every minute, so the file shows up in Inbox/Meetings within a minute. If the Mac was asleep, it comes in on the next pull.

Atlas never logs in to Gmail, Notion or the notetaker. If Notion's write fails, n8n stops there as it always has and nothing reaches the vault; if only the GitHub commit fails, Notion still has the meeting. The same meeting is never committed twice.

What's in a meeting file

At the top (frontmatter)

title, date, start (and end when known), kind (Notion's Category: Standup, Retro, 1:1…), provider (gemini, earlier granola), the provider's own id, and attendees — each a name and email. A list address like platform-team@… is kept as an attendee marked group: true, and never becomes a Person.

The sections, in this order

Summary, Notes (Gemini's Details), Provider next steps, Transcript. Any can be missing; there's never a section that isn't one of these four.

  • Provider next steps are kept exactly as the notetaker suggested them, one per line: - [Tobias Fenn] Flag the cache: put the new cache behind a flag before Friday. The owner is in brackets; Granola also adds (confidence: high), medium or low at the end. They're suggestions, not tasks — nothing becomes a task by itself.
  • Transcript is one paragraph per speaker turn, with a blank line between turns. Each turn is the speaker in bold, the time, the words, and a block id of its own at the end: 
    **Mara Quill** [~00:00:00] Morning. Shall we start with the cache? ^t0001

**Tobias Fenn** [~00:09:44] Yes. It's ready, but I'd like a flag on it first. ^t0002
    The id is what lets you link to or embed one exact turn — [[2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync#^t0002]] — and quote it in another note.
  • The time. [00:09:44] is the turn's own time; [~00:09:44] with a ~ is approximate — Gemini stamps sections, not turns, so each turn gets its section's time. Gemini's times count from the start of the recording; Granola's are the time of day.
  • Who spoke. A real name as the notetaker gave it; You, which is you — read as your name from Settings → Profile (with no name there it stays You); or Unknown for anyone the notetaker couldn't name (Granola's “Remote Speaker”). An Unknown speaker is never guessed into a Person.
  • Gemini's boilerplate — the disclaimer, “Chat with meeting transcript” links, the transcript's date and end lines — is left out.

Set it up

The full steps are in tools/n8n on GitHub (the README). In short, on your work computer's n8n:

  1. A GitHub token that can only write to pkm-space. A fine-grained token, Only select repositories → pkm-space, with Contents → Read and write and nothing else. It lives only in n8n's credential store, saved as GitHub pkm-space (contents) — never in the workflow, the vault or the Atlas repo.
  2. Paste the nodes. Copy tools/n8n/meeting-to-atlas.workflow.json, paste it into your Gemini → Notion workflow, and connect your Notion node's output to Meeting fields for Atlas. Rename your parse step to Parse meeting email so the new nodes can read it, and map its fields in Meeting fields for Atlas.
  3. Check the time zone. In Map meeting to Atlas file, timeZone is 'America/Los_Angeles'. Notion stores times as instants; this is the clock they're shown on, so a meeting lands on the right day and start time.
  4. Dry run first. Deactivate the two Commit meeting file nodes (D), run once on a real email, and read Map meeting to Atlas file's output: the date and start, the attendees, one turn per paragraph. Then reactivate them, run again, and look in pkm-space for the file.

Check a file by hand

To see whether Atlas will accept a meeting file — one from the dry run, or one you wrote — run the same check Atlas uses, in the Atlas folder:

pnpm validate:meeting "2026-09-29 Platform weekly sync.md"

ok (with the number of turns) means Atlas will accept it. FAIL lists each problem with where it is — for example attendees[0] must be a { name, email, group } entry, not a line of text.

Terms

Teaching Atlas how names are spelt. A notetaker mishears the names it doesn't know — a product, a colleague, a vendor. A term is the one right spelling and the ways it gets misheard, and Atlas and Claude both use the list.

Where it is

Sidebar

Terms, at the end of the rows at the top.

Search

⌘K → Terms.

Add a term

  1. On the Terms page, press New term.
  2. Right spelling: how it should be written, for example Larkspur.
  3. Misheard as: the ways it comes out wrong, commas between them — lark spur, Larks Burr. You can leave it empty and fill it in later.
  4. Kind: product, person, company, acronym or other — or leave it at —.
  5. Add term. It's listed as soon as Atlas has read it.

A term is a note of the Term type, made in Terms/ and named after its spelling: Terms/Larkspur.md. A spelling a file name can't hold, like S/4 Ledger, is kept as the note's title. Open a term from its name to add notes about it, or to set Refers to, the company it belongs to (where the vault's Term type has it — the Terms page works without the type's file). A spelling left empty is refused: “A term needs its right spelling.”

Change how it's misheard

Each term's Misheard as is a box on the page. Change it and press Return or click away: only the variants list at the top of the note changes, nothing else in it. Two spellings that differ only in capitals or spaces are one, kept as first typed.

What Atlas knows

  • Every term's right spelling and the ways it's misheard.
  • Every person's and company's name — a name is its own right spelling, so mara quill comes back as Mara Quill without a term — and their Also spelt spellings.
  • Capitals and spaces don't matter when Atlas matches: Lark  Spur and lark spur are one spelling. A longer name is tried before a shorter one inside it, so Larkspur Payroll wins over Larkspur.
  • The top of the page counts them: “2 terms · 9 spellings Atlas puts right, people and companies included”.
  • Templates, notes in hidden folders and archived notes don't count. To retire a term, archive it.

Conflicts

If two notes claim one spelling for two different right spellings — lark spur as a variant of both Larkspur and Larkspur Payroll — Atlas can't know which you meant, so it leaves that spelling alone. The page shows a Conflicts box above the list with each such spelling and the notes that claim it (click one to open it), and under the term: “In conflict, so not used: “lark spur””. Take the spelling off one of them and the conflict goes.

Built in

Term and Company are built-in types: Delete is turned off for both — “Term is built in — the Terms page lists its notes, and Atlas spells names by them — so it cannot be deleted.” Their properties are yours to change.

What's next. For now Atlas keeps the list; nothing is corrected yet. Next (P28-06): when a meeting arrives, its transcript is corrected by this list — every change recorded on the note, and undoable.

Tags

A tag is plain text in the note — #idea — read the same way when it is drawn, counted, clicked and renamed.

Write one

TypeIs
#ideaThe tag idea.
#tag me#A tag of several words, closed by a # later on the same line, as Bear writes it.
#area/topicA nested tag: topic under area. Nest as deep as you like.
# TitleA heading, not a tag — the space makes the difference.
tags: [idea, area/topic]Tags in the note's frontmatter count too, with or without the #.
  • A tag needs a letter in it: #1 stays a number. It is words of letters, numbers, _ or -.
  • A tag, and each part and word of it, can't start or end with _: #my_tag is fine, #_draft_ is no tag.
  • A one-letter word doesn't close a tag of several words, because its # is C# or F#: #todo fix the F# build is the tag todo.
  • The # must start the word: C#, issue#12 and a link's #heading are not tags, and neither is anything in code. Write \# to keep a # as plain text.
  • #Idea and #idea are one tag, shown as it was first spelled.

Suggestions as you type

  • After # and a letter, a list offers your tags with how often each is used. The tag you typed comes first — the one of that name, or Create #… if it is new — then longer and nested tags that match.
  • ↓ ↑ to choose another, Return or Tab to write it, Esc to close the list. For a new single word you can just keep typing.

The Tags page

  • Tags are highlighted in notes and on feed cards; click one to open it on the Tags page. Tags is also in the sidebar, under Graph.
  • The page lists every tag as a tree, nested tags under their parent, each with its count of uses. A parent's count includes its children's. Archived notes aren't counted. Sort by Name or Frequency.
  • Choose a tag to see the notes that use it, or any tag under it, with how many times; click one to open it.
  • There is nothing to clean up: a tag no note uses any more disappears from the list on its own.

Rename or merge

  • Choose a tag, then Rename. Type the New name and Preview lists the files that will change and how many uses in each. The button, now Rename n uses, changes it in every note, in the text and in tags: alike, touching nothing else in the file.
  • Tags nested under it follow: renaming #para to #area makes #para/resource into #area/resource.
  • If the new name is already a tag, the button reads Merge into #… and the two become one tag. A tag used only as the parent of others counts: renaming to #beta when #beta/y is written merges too.
  • Renaming to the same name with other capitals makes every use spelled the same way.
  • A rename reaches archived notes too, though the page doesn't count them.
  • A tag can't be moved inside itself (#para to #para/old), and a name that breaks the rules above is refused with the reason.
  • A new name of several words can't end in a one-letter word, since its closing # would read as C# does. plan a is refused: “A tag of several words can’t end in a one-letter word: #plan a# would read back as a shorter tag.”
  • Where the new name, closed with its #, would run into what follows it — #idea_ or #idea#x renamed to my tag — that note keeps the old name. The preview lists them after “1 note keeps #idea:”, each with why, such as “#my tag# can’t be written right before “_” and still read back as that tag.”
  • If another rename, in the app or over the API, has just made the new name a tag, the rename is refused at the last moment rather than merging unasked: “#… is in use already, so the rename would merge into it.” Preview again: the button will then read Merge into #….
  • A note with typing not yet saved is left alone; the message after the rename names it, so you can rename again once it is saved.
Filtering by tag. An Atlas query finds notes by tag, written in the text or in tags: alike: Tag in the builder, or tag = #idea as text, which also finds the tags nested under it. A type's own view filters only by a frontmatter tags: property, like any other property.

Automations

A rule that tidies the vault while you work: when it runs, which notes it takes — an Atlas query — and what it does to them, archive them or set a property. Every run is logged, can be tried first as a dry run, and the last one can be undone.

Where they are

  • Automations is in the sidebar, under Tags. It opens a page listing every rule.
  • Each rule is a note in .atlas/automations, under System in Pages. Its frontmatter is the rule; anything you write in its body is your own notes about it.

Start one

  • Archive done tasks after 30 days, the ready-made one, sits at the top of the page. It opens in the editor for you to look over; nothing is made until you press Create automation. It runs every day at 03:00, takes FROM task WHERE status = done, only notes not changed in the last 30 days, and archives them.
  • New automation starts a blank rule, taking every note of your first type.

Only notes not changed in the last … daysTick it to leave out notes changed recently, by their modified date: 1 to 3650 days.
DoArchive, or Set a property to a value: Property to Value.

Below the fields: Create automation or Save, Dry run, and, on a saved rule, Run now and Undo last run; Cancel or Close leaves it. A rule that can't be saved yet says why in red, like “Name the automation.” Under the buttons is the rule's Log, newest first.

The list

  • Each rule has a switch (Run <name> on its schedule), what it does and when (Archive · Every day at 03:00), Last run with what it did, Next run, and Run now.
  • Next run shows — for a rule that only runs when Atlas opens or by hand, one that is off, and one paused until it is run by hand.
  • Turning a rule on is logged as Turned on, and its schedule counts from then.
  • A rule held up says why beside it: “Paused: Its log could not be written (…). Run it by hand to try again.”, “Could not run (…); it is tried again at 14:05.”, or “Its log has an entry dated …, later than now; its schedule counts from before it.”
  • A rule whose file is wrong is listed too, never run, with why: “This rule’s file cannot be read: Write enabled: true or false.” Fix the file and it is read again.

Dry run, run, undo

  • Dry run says exactly what the rule would do now — “Would archive 12 notes.” or “Nothing to do right now.” — then lists each note it would take (click to open) and each it would leave, with why. It changes nothing, and works before a new rule is saved.
  • Run now runs it at once, on or off, whatever its schedule. Afterwards Last run says what it did: “Archived 12 notes. Left 1 alone.”
  • Undo last run undoes the newest run that did something, once. It can't reach further back: that would put back notes later runs have acted on since.
  • It reverses only what that rule did: notes it archived go back where they were, and properties it set go back to what they held.
  • What has changed since is left as it is and listed: a property changed again, a note moved out of where the run put it, or an archived note whose archive stamp isn't the run's.
  • The log is a note anyone could edit, so a line the rule couldn't have made — an archive in a rule that sets, a key it doesn't set, a value no property holds — is skipped and listed, never carried out.

When they run

  • Atlas checks the rules when a vault opens, once it is indexed, then every minute while it is open. Nothing runs while Atlas is closed.
  • A daily or hourly rule whose time passed while Atlas was closed catches up once when it opens, however many times it missed.
  • With sync on, rules run on their schedule on one Mac only — the one that set up sync first (a Mac connecting later never takes them), until you turn on Run automations on this Mac on another.
  • A daily or hourly rule that is on but has never run or been turned on here — written by hand, or synced in — is marked First seen in its log, and its schedule counts from then, so a daily rule runs by the next day.
  • A mark in the log dated later than now — a clock that ran ahead, or a log synced from one — is ignored, and the rule says so.
  • A rule that keeps failing waits longer each time before it is tried again: 1, 2, 4, 8… minutes, up to 6 hours. A run by hand tries it at once.
  • If its log can't be written, the rule is paused until you run it by hand, rather than change notes nothing records.
  • One run at a time; a save from the editor waits for a run under way, never dropped.

Safe by design

  • At most 500 notes are changed in one run. Past that it says “Stopped at 500, the most one run may do; the rest wait for the next run.” Notes already as the rule would leave them don't count.
  • A note open with unsaved typing is left as it is and logged: “It is open in Atlas with unsaved typing, so it was left as it is.”
  • A rule only acts on its own vault. Open another and the run stops: “Another vault was opened, so the automation stopped.” What it did up to then is logged.
  • An archive rule never touches notes already archived, even with INCLUDE ARCHIVED. It archives by the same rules as the app, updating links as it goes.
  • Atlas's own files in .atlas are never changed by a rule.
  • A connected Claude can read the rules and their logs and dry-run one, but never run, undo, turn on or off, make or change one — that stays on this page.

The rule's file

A rule can be written or edited by hand, anywhere. Its frontmatter:

atlas: automation
id: Archive done tasks after 30 days
name: Archive done tasks after 30 days
enabled: true
when: daily at 03:00
which: FROM task WHERE status = done
olderThanDays: 30
do: archive
KeyHolds
atlasautomation, which makes the note a rule.
idWhat its log is named after. Atlas writes it in when it first reads a rule without one, so renaming the rule keeps its history, and a new rule with a deleted one's name starts its own log. Two rules can't share one.
nameWhat the list calls it; the file's name if left out.
enabledtrue or false, nothing else — yes or 1 makes the rule broken rather than quietly off.
whendaily at HH:MM, every N hours (1 to 168), on app open, or manually.
whichAn Atlas query: FROM task WHERE …
olderThanDaysOptional: only notes not changed in this many days, 1 to 3650.
doarchive, or set with the values under set:, like set: {status: done}.
  • What set may write: plain values — text, a number, or true / false. 007 stays text, so a code keeps its zeros.
  • What it may not touch: type, id, any key starting with atlas, and the Archive's stamps archived, archivedFrom and archivedPrior: “A rule cannot set “type”: Atlas keeps that one itself.”

The log

  • One file per rule, .atlas/automations/log/<id>.md, headed <name> — run log. Each run, undo, Turned on and First seen is a ## section, newest last: when, what happened, then one line per note changed or left and why.
  • It's meant to be read. You can add your own sections, like ## Notes; Atlas keeps them.
  • It keeps the latest 100 entries, and no more than 256 KB; the oldest entries go first, never your own sections.
Not yet. Rules that run when a note changes. For now a rule runs on a schedule, when Atlas opens, or by hand.

Claude in Atlas

Ask Claude about your vault without leaving the app. It starts out knowing the page you're on, can search and read everything, and proposes changes you accept — it never writes on its own.

Open it

  • Press the spark button in any page's top bar (Ask Claude (⌘J)), or ⌘J. It opens as a pane beside your work; the same button or ⌘J closes it. Not while a dialog is open.
  • What you have open comes along as a chip above the box: a note (its title, properties and text), a view (its first 20 rows), a dashboard (its widgets), or, on the Query page in Atlas query, the query you're writing and its first rows. The × on the chip (Leave this out) drops it.
  • Nothing needs turning on first: the chat doesn't use the Local API, only the way to Claude chosen in Settings → Claude.

Ask

  • Type in Ask Claude… and press Return (⇧ Return for a new line). The answer streams in; Stop ends it.
  • Under an answer, Copy copies it; under the latest one, Retry asks again.
  • Answers show as plain text for now: markdown in them appears as written.

What Claude can do

  • Search and read everything with the same read-only tools a connected Claude has over MCP: notes, types, backlinks, views, dashboards, queries, calendars, tags and the Archive. Each call shows in the conversation as a line, like Searched for “…” or Ran the query “…”.
  • Archived notes come only when you ask about them.
  • It can't run automations, refresh sources or reach the web, and nothing it reads is changed.

Changes you accept

When you ask for a change, Claude proposes it as a card — Proposed edit or Proposed new note — showing the full vault path it would write, any property changes, and the text as the note would show it: removed blocks struck through, added ones marked, unchanged runs folded (3 unchanged blocks).

  1. Nothing is written until you press Accept; Reject drops it. The buttons grey out while it writes, so one Accept is one write.
  2. Accept writes exactly what the card showed, at exactly that path.
  3. Undo on an accepted card puts the note back as it was, if nothing has written it since.
When Accept refuses. If the note changed after the proposal, the card says <note> changed since this was proposed; nothing was written. If it's open with typing not yet saved: <note> has unsaved typing; save it first, so nothing you typed is written over. Nothing can be proposed into .atlas, any other hidden folder, or Chats/. A tool call Claude shows inside a code block is shown as an example, not run.
  • Cards sit in the conversation, which scrolls on its own; the window doesn't.
  • If a proposal can't be made, the conversation says so, with the reason: Could not propose an edit to <path> or Could not propose a new note, “<title>”. The chat's note keeps it as a line: > Could not propose an edit to X: <reason>.
  • A chat reopened from Past chats shows its past proposals as lines only, with no card. To get a card, ask again.

Chats

  • Each chat is saved as a note in Chats/, named from the first words you asked. After the first turn Atlas only adds to it, so anything you edit in an old chat is kept.
  • Past chats in the pane's header lists them and reopens one; New chat starts over.
  • If a chat can't be saved, the pane says The chat could not be saved to Chats/: … and the reason.

Your name (Settings → Profile)

Settings (⌘,) → Profile has two fields: Full name, the way you want it written, and Preferred name — What you go by, if it is not your first name. Press Return or leave the field to save it; Esc puts back what it held.

  • The name is kept in this vault's settings (.atlas/settings.md), not on this Mac, so every Mac that syncs the vault has it. Set it once.
  • Clearing a field removes the name; it isn't saved as a blank. Changing one leaves the other as the file holds it.
  • Invisible characters (zero-width spaces, direction marks and the like) and line breaks are dropped, and a name is cut at 100 characters, never halfway through an emoji or a flag.
  • Until the vault's settings have been read, both fields are greyed out and the line under Full name says Not read yet: Claude asks for your name. With no full name it says Not set: Claude writes “[Your name]” rather than guess one; with one, What Claude writes for you. If the name can't be read or saved, the reason shows under the fields.

What Claude does with it:

  • A full name is set: Claude uses exactly that name wherever a note needs yours — an owner, author, assignee, attendee or signature — never another spelling of it.
  • No full name: it writes [Your name] there, or asks you. A preferred name alone isn't enough for this.
  • Atlas can't read the name yet (the settings aren't read, or can't be): it asks you rather than writing [Your name], since you may well have set one.
  • It never guesses a name — not from your username, an email address, a folder path or anything else. A name set mid-chat counts from the next question.

While no full name is set — even if a preferred name is — an empty chat says Claude doesn’t know your name, so where a note needs it, it writes “[Your name]” rather than guess., with a Set your name button that opens Settings.

Set it up (Settings → Claude)

Settings (⌘,) → Claude → Reach Claude through: Claude Code (the default) or API key. The line under it says Ready, or what's wrong and the command that fixes it. The choice is kept on this Mac, not in the vault.

Outside data and secrets

A source brings data from outside the vault — a calendar feed, a CSV or JSON file or web address, or someone else's SQLite database — in as ordinary notes.

A source is a note in .atlas/sources (under System in Pages) whose properties say where to read, the format (ics, csv, json or sqlite), which folder to write into, which type the notes are, and which field becomes which property. The Atlas project's own vault has one, Milestones, to copy from.

  • Open the source note and press Refresh. It shows what it Reads, where it Writes to, how often it Refreshes (only when you ask, or every few minutes while the note is open), and what the last refresh did: created, refreshed, kept your text, no longer in the feed.
  • If you've edited a note since, your text is kept; only the fields the source owns are rewritten. A record that has gone from the feed marks its note — it is never deleted.
  • The folder it writes into must already exist.
Where a source may run. A plain public feed, or a file inside the vault, works from a note anywhere. A source that sends a secret, or reads a SQLite file outside the vault, runs only when its note is in .atlas/sources (System → sources in Pages). Anywhere else, Refresh fetches nothing and says This source sends the secret “github”, so it runs only from .atlas/sources. Move the note there (System → sources) to refresh it. — or, for a database, This source reads a database outside the vault, so it runs only from .atlas/sources. Move the note there (System → sources) to refresh it. That's because other tools, like the Local API or a synced folder, can write notes in your pages but not in .atlas.

Secrets (tokens for private feeds)

Some feeds need a key. Keep it in Settings (⌘,) → Secrets, which stores it in your Mac's Keychain — never in the vault — and a source names it rather than holding it: {{secret:github}} in its address or a header, or auth: { secret: github }.

  1. Under the list, fill in the three boxes — the name (e.g. github), the value, and the sites it may be sent to (e.g. api.github.com; several separated by commas) — and press Add. All three are needed.
  2. Each secret in the list says which sources use it and Sent only to … the sites you gave.
  3. Replace… changes the value, Sites… changes where it may go, Delete removes it from the Keychain (it tells you which sources will stop refreshing).

The same list holds the key for Claude by API key: a secret named anthropic, with the site api.anthropic.com. Atlas sends it only to Anthropic's API.

Why a secret can be paused. A source note is just text — something synced in, or a tool using the Local API, could have written it — so the note doesn't get to decide where your key goes. Atlas sends a secret only over https, only to the sites you bound it to, and won't follow a redirect to anywhere else. A secret with no sites (for example one set before this rule arrived) shows Sent nowhere until you choose the sites it is for and is refused until you press Sites… and add them. Adding a site to a key that's already saved asks you in a Mac dialog first.

A secret a source names but this Mac doesn't have shows as not set on this Mac, with Set…. Secrets belong to one vault on one Mac: a vault that's moved or copied to another Mac needs them set again. Atlas never shows a secret's value back, and strikes it out of anything a server echoes.

A SQLite file

  • A source with format: sqlite, a file: and a query: turns the query's rows into notes. The query follows the same read-only rules as SQL on the Query page.
  • The file is opened strictly read-only: Atlas never writes to it or leaves files beside it. A database that's being written to (unfinished changes still pending) or that changes while it's read is refused rather than read wrong — try again later.
  • A file outside the vault must be picked with Choose file… on the source note. That choice counts only on this Mac; elsewhere, choose it again.

Atlas has a local API and an MCP server, so Claude Code, Claude Desktop and scripts can search, read and write your vault while Atlas is open.

  1. Settings (⌘,) → Connections → turn on Local API. It's off until you do.
  2. Build the MCP server once: pnpm --filter @atlas/mcp build

What Claude can do besides reading and writing notes

  • Add a picture to a note (atlas_add_image). From a file, it reads only PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, SVG or HEIC images up to 20 MB in your Desktop, Downloads or Pictures folder, or the temporary folder — never anything else on the disk. To use other folders, set ATLAS_MCP_IMAGE_DIRS to them, separated by :; it replaces the list. The picture lands where pictures you add do, and a link to it goes at the end of the note.

  • Read a calendar view's days (atlas_calendar): which notes fall on which day of a saved view's calendar, and which have no date yet.
  • Refresh a source (atlas_refresh_source), as its Refresh button does — at most once every 30 seconds per source, and only where the source may run.
  • List tags and their notes (atlas_tags, atlas_tagged_notes), as the Tags page does. A tag used only in templates isn't listed.
  • Read how names are spelt (atlas_terms): every term, every spelling Atlas puts right with what it should be — people's and companies' names and their other spellings included — and the conflicts, which Claude is told never to correct. Read-only: Claude adds a term by making a note with type: term, and changes its misheard spellings as it changes any property.

  • Run a saved query view (atlas_run_view), the same as any view: it answers with the query's rows as the app finds them — the query's own INCLUDE ARCHIVED decides whether archived notes come too — and a query with a mistake is refused with the reason. Adding, renaming, copying, reordering and deleting views stay in the app.

Only the board's own columns and lanes are accepted; anything else is refused, naming them, so Done for done never makes a column the type doesn't have. A move that would finish a task needs the note's last-modified time, so a retried move can't finish a repeating task twice. Only a board's cards move — a view note can't be moved this way.
  • Add a note in a group (atlas_add_to_view), as + New in that group does: it gets the group's value, and the sub-group's, typed as the property stores them, and is numbered past any note of the same name. Only .md notes are made.
  • Run a query you'd build (atlas_run_query): the same queries as the builder or the text, read-only. It answers with the rows, and with the groups and sub-groups for GROUP BY, as the app groups them. A mistake comes back with its line and column. Archived notes come only when the query's text says INCLUDE ARCHIVED. Up to 5000 rows. Notes in .atlas or a hidden folder are never reached, not even through a relation. Saving a query as a view stays in the app.
  • Read automations (atlas_automations, atlas_automation_log, atlas_automation_dry_run): every rule with its schedule, last run, next run and why it's paused, rules whose file can't be read listed apart; a rule's log, newest first, up to 100 entries; and exactly what a run would do now, writing nothing — up to 500 notes, with how many more it would pass over.   • Read your name (atlas_profile): the full name and preferred name from Settings → Profile, and [Your name] to write when there's no full name. Claude is told to use exactly that name for an owner, author, assignee or attendee, and never to guess one from a username, an email address or a path. Read-only: the name is changed only in Settings. If the settings can't be read, it says so rather than answer “no name”.

Sync between Macs

Keep one vault the same on each of your Macs through a private GitHub repository. Atlas uses the GitHub login already on the Mac and never stores it.

Before you start (on each Mac)

  • In Terminal, run gh auth login, then gh auth setup-git. If they're missing, Atlas says so: “The GitHub command line isn’t logged in. In Terminal, run `gh auth login`, then try again.” (No gh at all: brew install gh first.)
  • The vault must not sit inside another git repository — such as the Atlas code folder. Atlas refuses: “This vault is inside another git repository (…), so Atlas won’t sync it: its notes would be mixed into that project. Move the vault out of that folder first.”

Set it up

  • Open the vault you want to sync, then Settings (⌘,) → Sync. Then one of: Your GitHub repositories — Search by name or owner, then Connect; Or create a new private repository — type a name, Set up sync with GitHub; Or paste an address — Connect existing repo.
  • Whichever you pick, the vault you have open becomes the repository, right where it is. Atlas never makes a subfolder for it, never copies it anywhere and never switches to another vault.
  • Made the repository yourself, say on github.com? Don't create another — use Connect existing repo (or Connect in the list) with its address.
  • The Mac asks first, in an Atlas sync dialog: “Connect this vault to …?” → Connect. Sync follows the repository's default branch, whatever it's called.
  • A repository that already has files is merged with the vault, not swapped for it — nothing is replaced. If both have Atlas settings and they differ, this vault's settings stay in force on the first connect, and GitHub's copy is kept under Sync conflicts/atlas/ (for example Sync conflicts/atlas/settings (conflict from Studio).md) for you to take what you want from.
  • Sync counts as set up only once the repository exists and the vault has been pushed to it; then Settings → Sync says “Sync set up: this vault now syncs with its GitHub repository.” If creating it fails, the vault stays not set up — fix what the message says and try again; what Atlas already wrote (a .gitignore, a first commit) is reused.

When set-up is refused

  • The vault already sends somewhere else (an Obsidian Git vault, say) and you chose a new repository: “This vault already sends to …, so Atlas won’t make a new repository for it. To sync with that one, use Connect existing repo with its address. To use a new one instead, remove the old origin first: in Terminal, in the vault’s folder, run `git remote remove origin`.”
  • The name really is taken on your GitHub account: “You already have a repository with that name on GitHub. Pick another name, or connect that one by its address.” You only see this when it's true — any other failure says what actually went wrong.
  • A merge is half done in the vault's folder: “The vault’s repository is in the middle of a merge. Finish it, then set up sync.”

On your other Mac

  • Click the vault's name at the top of the sidebar → Open a vault from GitHub…. Paste the address, then Choose folder…; Atlas copies the vault into that folder and opens the copy.
  • This is for getting a vault you don't have open — never for syncing the one you do (that's Settings → Sync). Pick the open vault, or a folder inside it, and it's refused: “That folder is inside …, the vault you have open, so the copy would land inside it and Atlas would open the copy instead of your notes. To sync this vault with a GitHub repository, use Settings → Sync and connect it there. To open a vault from GitHub, choose a folder outside this one.” (Picking the vault itself starts “… is the vault you have open”; a shortcut or symlink to it is caught too.)
  • An empty repository is refused before anything is copied: “This repository is empty — there’s no vault in it to open. To put the vault you have open in it, use Settings → Sync and connect it there.”

While you work

  • Your changes are sent about 30 seconds after you stop — typing, an accepted Claude edit, an automation run, a move — and at least every 5 minutes while you keep working.
  • Every minute Atlas checks for other Macs' changes and brings them in; while you're typing, it waits until you stop. Unsaved typing is never overwritten; an open note reloads as after an outside edit.
  • Adjust both in Settings → Sync: When to send changes and How often to look for other Macs’ changes. Pause sync on this Mac stops it here only; Sync now syncs at once, paused or not.
  • A light at the bottom of the sidebar shows how it stands: Synced, Syncing…, N changes to bring in, N conflicts, Sync failed or Sync paused. Click it for Settings → Sync.
  • Closing the window syncs once more (waiting up to 20 seconds); anything left goes next time the vault opens.

What syncs

  • Everything in the vault: notes, pictures, .atlas (types, views, dashboards, templates, sources, automations and their logs, settings) and Chats/.
  • Not: secrets (each Mac's Keychain — set them on each Mac), the index (.atlas-cache/, rebuilt on each Mac), the Activity log, and window state.
  • Left out with a warning, and listed in Settings → Sync under Staying on this Mac, not synced:
    • a file of about 95 MB or more — “… is over GitHub’s 100 MB limit, so it isn’t synced. It stays on this Mac; to share it, make it smaller or keep it outside the vault.”
    • a folder that is a git repository of its own — “… is a git repository of its own, so only that folder isn’t synced: it stays on this Mac, and the rest of the vault still syncs. If it doesn’t belong in the vault, move it out.”
    • anything of .atlas that a line in your own .gitignore keeps out — “… is left out by a line in this vault’s .gitignore, so it isn’t synced and other Macs won’t get it. Atlas leaves your .gitignore as you wrote it; to sync it, remove that line.” Atlas never edits your lines; removing it is up to you.

When both Macs changed a file

  • Your version stays in place. The other Mac's is saved beside it as <name> (conflict from <Mac>).md; one of Atlas's own files (under .atlas) goes to Sync conflicts/atlas/ instead, so it isn't read as a second live rule or view.
  • Settings → Sync says “Both Macs changed a file. This Mac’s version was kept; the other Mac’s is saved beside it.”, and Activity gets a warning line. Nothing is lost, and git's conflict markers never land in a note.
Automations run on one Mac. Scheduled automations run on one Mac only, so two Macs never run the same rule. The first Mac to set up sync gets them; a second Mac connecting later — even through Connect existing repo — leaves them where they are. To move them, turn on Run automations on this Mac in Settings → Sync on the Mac you want.
No tokens. Atlas never stores a GitHub token. An address with one in it is refused: “That address has a password or token in it. Use the plain address from GitHub’s Code button: this Mac’s own GitHub login is what signs in.”

Your files

  • Every note, type, view and dashboard is a markdown file in your vault. Atlas writes back only what you changed; everything else keeps its exact bytes, so Obsidian and git see clean edits.
  • Links are stored as [[Name]], the same way Obsidian writes them. A bookmark is the same link alone on its line followed by <!-- atlas:bookmark -->, a comment Obsidian's reading view and GitHub hide; nothing else about the card is stored.
  • A block link or embed is [[Note#^id]] or ![[Note#^id]], with the ^id written after its block, as Obsidian writes it — see Blocks and embeds. A - [ ] line with nothing after it is an empty task.
  • Tags are stored as you typed them, #tag in the text or a tags: list in the frontmatter. Obsidian reads single-word and nested tags the same way; it reads #tag me# as #tag followed by text.
  • Archived notes are ordinary files in Archive/, at the path they had, with archived and archivedFrom in their frontmatter, so Obsidian still opens them.
  • Frontmatter written flow-style, {status: done, tags: [a]}, and files that start with a byte-order mark are kept intact when Atlas changes a property.
  • .atlas/ holds types, templates, views, dashboards, sources, automations and settings. It shows as System in Pages — all but the templates, which are on the Templates page, and the views and dashboards, which have their sections.
  • A template is a plain markdown file in .atlas/templates/, named for what it makes: Person.md, Daily.md, Task.md, Artifact.md.
  • Each automation is a note in .atlas/automations/, and its run log a note in .atlas/automations/log/, named after the rule's id. Both are plain markdown you can read, diff and edit.
  • The Activity log is not in the vault: one file per vault in Atlas's data folder on this Mac, about 30 days or 5 MB.
  • A link (symlink) in your vault that points into .atlas or .git doesn't open — Atlas refuses it, so nothing can reach Atlas's own files by a side door.
  • The search index lives in .atlas-cache/. It's only a copy; if it's ever wrong, Settings → Index → Rebuild.
  • Pictures you add go in attachments/ (or beside the note, if you chose that in Settings → Images). Thumbnails Atlas makes of pages live in .atlas-cache/thumbnails; an artifact's is kept with its saved copy.
  • Each chat with Claude is a note in Chats/, written once and then only added to. Claude's proposed changes are written only when you press Accept, never into .atlas, a hidden folder or Chats/.
  • A synced vault is a git repository; Atlas keeps its own lines in .gitignore (the index, Finder and Obsidian per-Mac files). See Sync between Macs.
  • Secrets live in the macOS Keychain, never in the vault. The chat's settings (which way to reach Claude, the model) are kept on this Mac; your name is in the vault's settings, so it syncs.
  • Nothing leaves your Mac unless you turn on the Local API, connect a source, turn on sync (the vault goes to your GitHub repository), or ask Claude in the chat — then what you ask, the page on the chip and what Claude reads go to Anthropic, through Claude Code or your API key.
